How it works

Each map cell (about 8 km) runs every species model day by day. Temperatures come from NOAA nClimGrid-Daily, which reaches about three days behind today. The National Blend of Models, the National Weather Service's gridded forecast, fills those days and the next ten. After that, predictions assume the 1991–2020 normal for each day and place.

Predicted dates are marked observed, provisional (NWS analysis of the last few days), forecast or projected (normal temperatures), so you can see how firm each date is.

Drivers

HeatDegree-days above a base temperature since Jan 1, or since a day-length cue (brown marmorated stink bug)
Day lengthExact day length for each latitude, used as a start cue and for autumn senescence
ChillingCool degree-days after a date or once days shorten, for fall leaf color
RainRolling rainfall totals, for rain-triggered green-up (buffelgrass)

Where the models come from

  • Published models: DDRP parameter files for spotted lanternfly, emerald ash borer, Asian longhorned beetle, Japanese beetle and light brown apple moth; extension models for brown marmorated stink bug; the USA-NPN rain rule for buffelgrass.
  • Fitted models: for other species, onset, peak and end thresholds are fitted to research-grade iNaturalist photos annotated with life stage or flowering, fruiting and leaf state. The base temperature or chilling rule is picked by cross-validation and tested on held-out recent years. A model is shown only if it beats a single calendar date.
  • Every model is checked against iNaturalist records; the panel beside the map reports how.

Limits

  • Contiguous US only. The cells average terrain, so sunny slopes and cities run ahead of the map.
  • iNaturalist records lean toward cities, roadsides and weekends. Fitted dates describe when people photograph a stage, which tracks but can trail the biology.
  • “Only where recorded” uses iNaturalist records within about 25 km. A missing record does not mean a species is absent.
  • Use the dates to plan scouting and treatment, and confirm stages in the field.
